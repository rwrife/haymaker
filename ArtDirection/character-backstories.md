# Haymaker character backstories

Story reference for the four supplied T-pose character models. These stories guide future career scenes, dialogue, and animation; they do not change the current playable roster. Goldie and Nico retain their established concept backgrounds. Mack and Bruiser's histories are new story proposals for the player and final boss.

## Mack — the player

Mack grew up above a neighborhood boxing gym where the lights stayed on long after the last paying member went home. While other kids watched championship highlights, Mack swept the canvas, patched heavy bags, and listened to the old fighters argue about footwork. The gym's owner taught Mack one rule: a hard punch gets attention; knowing when to throw it wins the fight.

Now an adult working early shifts at a delivery depot, Mack trains whenever the gym is quiet. When rising costs threaten to close the place, Mack enters the Haymaker circuit, hoping the prize money will buy the gym another year and a championship will bring people through its doors. There is no famous surname, polished entourage, or shortcut up the ladder—just a pair of green gloves and a habit of getting back to work.

Mack starts out trying to prove that determination can solve every problem. Each opponent teaches a different lesson: pace, patience, deception, restraint. By the final bout, Mack understands that saving the gym means carrying its lessons forward, not simply hitting harder than everyone else.

**Personality:** earnest, stubborn, quietly funny; respects opponents without being intimidated by them.

**Fighting identity:** adaptable fundamentals; learns tells, manages stamina, and turns small openings into clean combinations.

**Voice:** “One round at a time.”

## Goldie Voltage

Goldie repairs arcade cabinets in a workshop crowded with spare buttons, tangled wiring, and machines everyone else has given up on. She loves the moment a dead cabinet lights up again. After closing, she boxes at a nearby gym, chasing the same satisfaction: find the broken rhythm, make the right adjustment, and get everything moving.

She entered the Haymaker circuit to fund a traveling arcade that could bring restored games to neighborhood fairs. The prize matters, but so does the crowd. Goldie wants a bout to feel like the best night at an arcade: loud, fast, and worth another try. Her grin is an invitation to keep up, even while she is landing a relentless combination.

Goldie sometimes mistakes constant motion for control. When someone survives her first rush, she has to resist the urge to throw even more punches and start reading the fight herself. She treats Mack as a welcome challenge and, between bouts, the first person she would recruit to help haul a cabinet up a flight of stairs.

**Personality:** enthusiastic, mischievous, generous; competitive without being cruel.

**Fighting identity:** relentless combination puncher; builds pressure in bursts and leaves openings when she overcommits.

**Voice:** “Still got another credit in you?”

## Nico “Nightshift” Navarro

Nico drives the last bus across the city, carrying kitchen crews, cleaners, and tired workers home while the bright parts of town go dark. Years on that route have taught him to notice the smallest warning: a change in someone's posture, a hesitation at a crossing, a turn taken too quickly. In the ring, he watches just as closely.

He trains after his shift in a nearly empty gym, then sleeps through the afternoon. The scuffed purple gloves and heavy-lidded eyes make him easy to underestimate. Nico does not waste energy correcting that impression. He lets impatient fighters spend their strength, waits for the commitment, and answers with a precise counter.

Nico entered Haymaker to win enough breathing room to reduce his shifts and finally train on a proper schedule. He has become so used to waiting for the safe moment that taking a chance on himself is his hardest fight. Mack earns his respect by learning to slow down; Nico, in turn, has to learn when waiting becomes hiding.

**Personality:** observant, understated, dryly funny; looks tired, stays composed.

**Fighting identity:** economical counterpuncher; punishes reckless attacks and makes opponents create their own openings.

**Voice:** “You were in a hurry. I wasn't.”

## Bruiser Baxter — the final boss

Before the spotlights and gold-trimmed gear, Baxter was the dependable heavyweight at a small gym: the fighter who stayed late to spar with beginners and helped carry equipment after exhibitions. His first championship changed the size of the crowds. His next few changed what he thought they expected of him.

“Bruiser” became a performance as much as a nickname. The swept hair, red-and-gold gloves, and thunderous entrances promised a champion nobody could move. Baxter learned to give the audience that champion, then began to believe he could never show them anything else. Every successful defense made the belt heavier.

At the top of the Haymaker ladder, Baxter sees Mack as an earnest newcomer who has not yet learned what winning costs. He means to deliver that lesson with overwhelming pressure. But Mack's connection to a neighborhood gym reminds him of the fighter he was before protecting his reputation became more important than enjoying the sport.

The final bout tests everything Mack has learned: readable power, feints, changing rhythms, and the discipline to wait through a storm. Baxter fights hard and fairly. When beaten, he offers a glove touch without excuses; losing the belt gives him permission to become a boxer again. His next visit to Mack's gym is to help, not to headline.

**Personality:** theatrical, proud, intimidating; beneath the showmanship, a generous mentor afraid of becoming irrelevant.

**Fighting identity:** heavyweight pressure backed by experience; mixes big windups, feints, guarded pauses, and punishing counters.

**Voice:** “You want this corner? Earn it.”
